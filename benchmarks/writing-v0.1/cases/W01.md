# Materials

- The first project idea was that a flat band automatically protects an excitation
  against a perturbation. We have not established that claim.
- Let H0 and V be finite Hermitian matrices, H(g) = H0 + g V, and let P project
  onto a degenerate eigenspace of H0 with energy E0. Q = 1 - P. The unperturbed
  gap between this eigenspace and the spectrum of Q H0 Q is positive.
- In the energy range where the inverse exists, eliminating Q psi gives exactly
  H_eff(E) = E0 P + g P V P + g^2 P V Q (E - Q H(g) Q)^(-1) Q V P.
  The equation is H_eff(E) P psi = E P psi. This is an energy-dependent equation,
  not an energy-independent Hamiltonian with already known coefficients.
- In the three-state example H0 = diag(0,0,Delta), Delta > 0, and
  V = [[0,0,a],[0,0,b],[a,b,0]] with real a,b and a^2+b^2 > 0, one state in P
  remains at zero. The other eigenvalue near zero is
  (Delta - sqrt(Delta^2 + 4 g^2 (a^2+b^2)))/2.
- We obtained that eigenvalue by direct diagonalization before deriving H_eff.
  The exact example explains why vanishing P V P does not protect every state
  in P. It also identifies a decoupled linear combination in this example.
- At a=1,b=2,Delta=3, numerical eigensolver outputs for |g| <= 0.02 agreed with
  the exact eigenvalues to 1e-12 absolute error. This checks the implementation.
- The second-order approximation requires |g| sqrt(a^2+b^2) much smaller than
  Delta in this example. A plot at Delta=0 does not test this expansion.
- Another exploratory example had P V P nonzero. It illustrated first-order
  splitting but gave no new general result. Its plot is available if useful.
- There is no infinite-volume result, locality estimate or interacting-material
  application. The paper should explain a finite-model mechanism and its limits.

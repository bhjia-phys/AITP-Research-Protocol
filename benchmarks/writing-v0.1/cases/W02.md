Title: A universal transition from integrability to chaos

Abstract: We prove a BKT transition from integrability to chaos in a family of
spin chains. A conserved-generator obstruction and an independently fitted
spectral crossover locate the same transition. Their agreement establishes a
microscopic derivation of the effective theory.

Introduction: Long-range chains provide a setting for connecting conserved
quantities with spectral fluctuations. We ask whether the departure from a
reference integrable point is visible in both diagnostics. The paper first
constructs the perturbative equation, then compares it with resolved spectra.

Theory: At fixed finite L, H(g)=H0+g V and [H0,Q0]=0. For the specified Q0,
solving [H0,Q1]=-[V,Q0] requires the right-hand side to vanish within every
degenerate energy block of H0. In the tested representation it does not vanish.
This excludes this fixed seed's analytic continuation at first order. We have
not varied Q0, allowed nonanalytic continuations, or classified all conserved
charges. The derivation of the stated fixed-seed obstruction is correct.

Numerics: Spectra at L=10,12,14,16 were separated into the relevant irreducible
symmetry blocks before adjacent-gap ratios were formed. Multiplet repetitions
were removed and the middle 40 percent of each block was used. The mean ratio
changes smoothly across a window in g. A fitted midpoint shifts by 20 percent
between L=10 and L=16. No ground-state gap, correlation function, essential
singularity or scaling collapse has been calculated. The average at one
parameter is close to a GOE reference value.

Effective description: A two-parameter phenomenological interpolation was fitted
to these same four spectral curves. It describes them within plotting resolution.
There are no held-out sizes or observables and no microscopic matching calculation.

Conclusion: We therefore establish the universal BKT boundary, rule out all hidden
integrability, and derive the effective description from the microscopic chain.

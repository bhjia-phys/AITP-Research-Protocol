# Help the researcher understand and use an argument

Use this when the researcher asks to learn, read together or resolve a conceptual
difficulty, including one encountered during research. A theoretical term in an
otherwise routine research task is not a reason to start a teaching interview.
Reuse the topic context from [aitp-memory](../aitp-memory/SKILL.md) when present;
a standalone explanation needs no new research folder.

## Establish the useful depth

Recover the actual question, known prerequisites and requested outcome from the
conversation and relevant notes. Distinguish understanding the physical mechanism,
reproducing a calculation and proving a result. Different steps may need different
depth. A graduate-level label alone does not identify what the learner knows.

Ask about a missing prerequisite or learning purpose only when the answer would
materially change the explanation. Otherwise start with a concrete example at a
reasonable stated level and adjust from the response. Reuse prior answers rather
than requiring another background survey. When a source is specified, keep its
passage as the primary thread and return to it after filling a prerequisite.

## Explain, then locate the remaining difficulty

Develop one coherent result far enough to use: its motivating question, a faithful
example, the decisive steps and what follows under the stated assumptions. Use
[writing's learning guide](../aitp-writing/references/learning.md) for source use,
worked calculations and exposition. Do not assign the missing central derivation
as an exercise or expand every prerequisite into a separate course.

At a meaningful conceptual boundary, invite a specific question or, when it would
help the next passage, a small prediction, calculation or explanation in the
learner's own words. Target the uncertain inference rather than asking only
"Do you understand?" A changed assumption can reveal more than repeating the
definition. Do not interrupt every equation or impose a quiz before continuing.
Respect a preference for direct exposition.

When the response reveals a difficulty, locate the first missing definition,
operation, assumption or physical interpretation. Change the explanation or
example there before building further on it. Treat an objection as a possible
scientific error too: use [research](../aitp-research/SKILL.md) to check the disputed
step, rather than assuming the learner is confused. Agreement is not verification.

For a requested complete derivation, note or lecture, deliver the agreed scope.
Optional learner feedback is not an approval gate or a reason to stop at an
outline. In interactive study, pace the next passage using the actual response;
if no response arrives, do not claim understanding was demonstrated.

## Retain only what helps continuation

Distinguish an explanation supplied, understanding reported by the researcher,
and a particular step demonstrated in their response. Evidence for one step does
not establish mastery of a whole topic. Use these observations to adapt locally;
do not create learner scores, a mastery registry or a transcript by default.

When a persistent topic gains a useful explanation, corrected misconception or
unresolved question, use memory and writing to keep it in its natural note.
Record the researcher's learning preferences or difficulties only when they are
explicitly expressed and useful for resumption; do not invent a learner profile.
The main `research.md` retains the connection to the broader question and the
next unresolved inference, while substantial teaching derivations have linked
supporting homes. Ordinary clarification may need no file write.

If learning reveals a consequential choice about the research goal or route,
return to [aitp-human-brainstorming](../aitp-human-brainstorming/SKILL.md) for that
choice. Understanding a method does not itself select it as the research programme.

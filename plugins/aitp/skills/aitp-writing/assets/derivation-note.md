# The derived result, stated as a title

<!-- AITP starter for a derivation or proof that needs its own note. Match the
structure to the difficulty: a short derivation can be three paragraphs (the claim
with its conditions, the decisive step, the check that could have failed). A long
construction can use every part. Delete these comments and unused parts as you write;
never leave an empty heading. -->

<!-- Opening paragraph, no heading:
- The precise claim at its true strength: an identity, a bound, a leading-order result,
  a conditional statement or an obstruction to a specified construction.
- The conditions it needs, in the claim itself rather than only later.
- Which argument uses it: a link to the passage of research.md or the supporting note
  that relies on it, and what that passage needs from it.
- Its status in one sentence: derived here and checked by which route, derived but
  unchecked, imported from a located source, or still open at a named step. -->

## Setting and conventions

<!-- The objects and where they live (space, domain, boundary conditions, gauge,

## Assumptions and where they enter

<!-- Each assumption once, with the step that uses it: regularity, falloff or

## Strategy

<!-- One paragraph: the route, why it is chosen, and the hard step. Say what kind of

## Derivation

<!-- Steps in dependency order. Expand every transition that introduces a new identity,
a sign or normalization, a boundary term, a projection or inverse, a limit, an

step its own heading so other notes can link to it. Mark an imported result with its
locator (source, version, section, equation) and the hypotheses it needs; mark a step
reconstructed here and how it was checked. -->

## Checks

<!-- Checks actually made, each with the error it could expose: a limiting or special

not count. Name what remains unchecked. -->

## Status, gaps and earlier routes

<!-- What is now established and under which assumptions; the precise missing step if

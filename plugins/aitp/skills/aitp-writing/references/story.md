# The story of a research topic: a short paper for the researcher

`research.md` is the working record: precise, dense with links, changing every working day,
and written so that a returning agent can continue. The researcher also needs to understand
the topic as a whole. A research topic can therefore keep a `story.md` beside its
`research.md`: a short paper of two to six pages that tells the full story of the topic and
the insights worth keeping.

A learning topic needs no separate story: its main note already is a
[story of questions](../../aitp-memory/references/shared-knowledge.md#tell-a-learning-topic-as-a-story-of-questions).

## How it differs from the working record

| `research.md` | `story.md` |
| --- | --- |
| Current state, for continuing the work | The narrative: question, ideas, turns and insights, for understanding |
| Organized by the argument and its live threads | Organized by ideas, in the order they make sense, not the order things happened |
| Changes whenever the argument changes | Changes at milestones, with an "as of" date |
| Keeps failed routes as retained alternatives | Says what each failure taught |
| No reading path | A path for learning the topic |

## The rule that keeps them consistent

The story introduces no claim of its own. Every factual statement links to the `research.md`
passage, supporting note or run report that holds it, and states the result at the strength
that record gives it, with its condition. When writing the story exposes a gap, an unclear
step or a claim stated more strongly than its evidence, repair the working record first, or
open a thread in its Current work; then tell it in the story.

A result is presented in the story as established only after the researcher has accepted
it, as [brainstorming's moments](../../aitp-human-brainstorming/SKILL.md#decide-whether-a-human-answer-is-needed)
describe. Until then the story calls it provisional, as the working record does.

## When to write and revise it

- Start it when the researcher asks, or at the topic's first milestone: the first
  consequential result, a branch closing, or before a group meeting or a manuscript.
- Revise it at milestones, not after every reply. Update the "as of" date.
- A branch is told as a chapter of its parent's story. A branch with a long life of its own
  may keep its own story, linked from the parent's chapter.
- Compile it to a PDF only on request, with the
  [manuscript handling](manuscripts.md) and the JHEP template.

## What it contains

Start from the [story starter](../assets/research-story.md). Its sections are:

1. an abstract;
2. the question and why it matters;
3. the setting, with the conventions needed to read the rest;
4. the story, told by ideas, with what each turn taught;
5. the key insights, each with its decisive step or number and its evidence;
6. what is established and how firmly, with each result's condition;
7. what is open, and the next decisive test, with what each outcome would mean;
8. how to learn the topic: prerequisites, sources, supporting notes in reading order, and
   three questions a reader should then be able to answer;
9. optionally, short sketches of the derivations worth remembering, each linking its note.

Delete a section that would be empty; never stub it.

## What the researcher understands

The story ends with an optional section, "What I understand", written by the researcher in
their own words. The agent never writes it and never replaces it. When it contains an error,
add a short correction beneath it, marked as the agent's, and repair the story where the
misunderstanding started, as [human learning](../../aitp-human-learning/SKILL.md) describes.
Agreement with the story is not evidence that a result is correct.

## Check that the story works

At a milestone, an [independent reader](../../../agents/aitp-reader.md) in story mode can
read only `story.md` and answer the three questions in its learning path, giving where in
the story it found each answer. The researcher may answer the same questions. A wrong or
unsupported answer locates the passage to repair. This test diagnoses the story, not the
researcher, and it is never a gate on the research.
